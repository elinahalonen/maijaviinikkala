# Maija Viinikkala | Brand and social media guidelines

**Status:** Approved visual direction, 10 October 2026. This is a **launch-ready brand and copy direction** for a website that will go public after Maija has completed her training. Business facts, services, and credentials require final verification before publication.

## 1. The identity in one paragraph

A warm, light and welcoming identity for a personal massage practice. Rich raspberry gives the brand individuality and a quietly feminine character; warm ivory keeps the experience spacious and fresh; antique gold adds warmth; botanical green introduces a natural counterweight without dominating. The result should feel personal and reassuring, but also visibly professional. Think sunlight beside water, gentle greenery and human presence, rather than a dense woodland retreat or a mystical spa.

**Core impression for a first-time visitor:** “I could feel comfortable here, and this practitioner seems approachable and competent.”

**Working name:** Maija Viinikkala

**Working descriptor:** Hierontaa ja hyvinvointia

**Launch-time framing:** This site is being built privately **before** Maija completes her training and will be published **after** she qualifies. Write all public-facing website and social copy as a professional practice operating now: "Tarjoan hierontaa", "Varaa aika", "Hierontapalvelut". Do **not** describe Maija as a trainee, newly qualifying, about to graduate, or not yet offering massage services. These are project-timing facts, not brand messages.

**Accuracy gate before publication:** Confirm the exact Finnish professional title she is entitled to use, completed qualifications and the treatments she will offer at launch. Do not invent credentials or specialisms. Pregnancy massage may be among her eventual services, and breastfeeding advice is a longer-term possibility; display either as a live service only if Maija confirms it is qualified and available at launch.

## 2. Colour palette

| Colour | Hex | Role | Where to use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raspberry | `#8D3558` | Primary signature | Main buttons, selected headings, navigation highlights, post headlines |
| Warm ivory | `#F8F5F0` | Primary background | Most pages, social post backgrounds, whitespace |
| Antique gold | `#CFA251` | Restrained accent | Thin rules, small ornaments, subtle borders, small graphic details |
| Botanical green | `#5A6B56` | Balancing secondary | Occasional panels, some cards, small botanical details, secondary social templates |
| Pale clay | `#E9D4C9` | Soft support | Gentle content backgrounds, cards, subtle social media panels |
| Deep plum | `#352A35` | Dark anchor | Main text, dark sections, headings and footers |

**Colour balance:** Let warm ivory and photographs do most of the visual work. Raspberry should be the most recognisable colour. Green should feel like a welcome addition, not the main environment. Gold is a detail, never an all-over metallic effect. Pale clay is optional. Do not try to make all six colours equally visible on every screen or post.

**Contrast and accessibility:** Raspberry on ivory (about 6.9:1), botanical green on ivory (about 5.3:1), and plum on ivory (about 12.6:1) work for normal-sized text. White on raspberry (about 7.6:1) and white on green (about 5.7:1) also work. Gold on ivory is low contrast (about 2.2:1), so do not use it for body text or essential labels. Gold on plum is more readable (about 5.8:1). Verify contrast if shades are changed, and do not use colour alone to convey meaning.

## 3. Typography

- **Headings and expressive moments:** Lora (regular, sometimes italic). Elegant and human, but not ornate or calligraphic.
- **Body text, navigation, labels and buttons:** Lato (regular, medium and semibold). Clear and contemporary.
- Large, generously spaced headlines; normal readable paragraph sizes and comfortable line spacing.
- Use italics sparingly, perhaps for a very short pull quote or one word. Avoid widespread ALL CAPS, widely spaced letters on long sentences, and multiple decorative fonts.
- Use Finnish characters correctly (ä, ö). Do not automatically force Finnish navigation labels into all-caps microtype.

## 4. Website design direction

**Preferred structure:** Calm editorial design with clear navigation, ample light space, friendly professional language, and a good portrait of Maija. Use imagery to create the fresh, sunny, natural atmosphere. Keep decorative elements sparse.

Suggested starting homepage:

1. Simple header: Maija Viinikkala, a clear descriptor, navigation, and one booking/contact action.
2. Hero with genuine photograph of Maija, a clear explanation of the practice, and a direct call to action.
3. Services in plain language, written as available treatments at launch, including who each is for, duration, price and what to expect. Use placeholders privately until Maija confirms details.
4. Short introduction to Maija as the practising professional, her qualifications and working approach, using verified facts.
5. Practical information: location, appointments, accessibility, cancellation terms, and contact details when available.
6. An uncomplicated footer.

Do not bury what she offers under abstract wellness language. The visitor should be able to tell quickly that this is a massage practice, who provides the treatments, where it is and how to enquire or book.

**Buttons:** Raspberry fill with white text for the primary action. Botanical green with white text is an acceptable secondary variant when needed. Use antique gold mainly as a tiny decorative detail, not the primary button colour. Consistent rounded corners, but avoid bubbly or overly playful controls.

**Layout:** Large margins, measured spacing, a small number of distinctive sections, photographic texture, thin accent rules. The page can be feminine without being frilly.

## 5. Photography and illustrations

**Seek:** Natural light, genuine portraits of Maija, an inviting treatment space, sunlight on water, soft greenery, warm textiles, small human details, subtle movement. The emotional reference is leaving a massage feeling lighter and refreshed. Photos of the actual practice should ultimately take priority over stock or AI-generated scenes.

**Use cautiously:** Botanical line drawings and tiny gold accents. A restrained drawing of a leaf or wildflower could be a useful recurring detail, but does not have to appear on every section.

**Avoid:** Obvious lotus motifs, the triple moon tattoo symbol, celestial branding, incense imagery, crystal grids, yoga-spiritual clichés, glitter-gold textures, stacked pebbles, heavily filtered fantasy portraits or a dark, enclosed woodland atmosphere. These were specifically not the direction Maija chose for her professional site. Personal symbolism need not become a logo.

**Important:** Existing moodboard portraits are AI-generated concepts, not authentic photographs of Maija. They should not be published as documentary images of her or the practice. Arrange for her to choose or take real photographs for the live site and obtain consent for any identifiable clients in images.

## 6. Tone of voice (Finnish)

Friendly, matter-of-fact, attentive and reassuring. Professional without sounding medicalised or distant. Warm without spiritual slogans or inflated promises. Write directly to the client using ordinary Finnish, and explain treatments clearly.

Useful tone examples (illustrations, **not approved service claims**):

- “Mitä ensimmäisellä hierontakäynnillä tapahtuu?”
- “Hierontaan saa tulla juuri sellaisena kuin on.”
- “Tutustu palveluihin” / “Ota yhteyttä” / “Varaa aika”, only if a booking route exists.

Avoid unsupported promises about pain relief, pregnancy outcomes, breastfeeding outcomes or medical efficacy. Write with confidence about professional massage services; do not narrate the pre-launch training timeline. Verify the official title and specialist qualifications **before the site goes live**.

## 7. Social media visual identity

The social feed should look like it belongs to the website while remaining more conversational and less formal. The rule is **consistent visual grammar, not identical templates repeated forever**.

### Formats

- Feed posts and carousels: create primarily at **1080 × 1350 px (4:5)** for portrait posts, checking crop previews for each platform.
- Stories: **1080 × 1920 px (9:16)**, with essential text and controls kept well away from the top and bottom interface areas.
- Profile image: clear natural portrait of Maija is preferable to a complicated miniature logo.
- Export sharp text and high-quality photographs. Avoid tiny writing that becomes unreadable on a phone.

### Website copy source

Use **`MAIJA_WEBSITE_COPY.md`** for the full Finnish website draft and short social launch examples. It is written as a practising professional’s website, ready for launch when the business opens. Keep the copy warm, direct and service-specific; do not add any public language about preparing for graduation. The separate copy file also holds editorial placeholders for location, contact information, pricing and Maija-approved service names.

### Four repeatable post families

**A. Educational / FAQ carousel**  
Warm ivory background, raspberry headline, plum body text, small gold line, possibly a green detail. Lead with a real client question. Keep one useful idea per slide; give practical details without overclaiming.

**B. Service introduction / booking information**  
A genuine Maija or practice photo with clean ivory or pale clay text space. Raspberry call-to-action. State what the service is, who it is for, and how to enquire/book. Use only verified offerings and pricing.

**C. Personal / behind-the-scenes**  
Real photo of Maija, her workspace, professional approach or an ordinary moment connected to her practice. Minimal graphics. Let her personality come through in the caption, not an overdecorated template.

**D. Short reassurance / seasonal note**  
Botanical green or pale clay panel, a brief readable line in plum or ivory/white as appropriate, and one small botanical or gold detail. Avoid turning every post into an inspirational quotation. Do not turn moon-phase or spiritual imagery into the central business identity.

### Social media composition rules

- Use **one strong accent colour per post** plus neutral space and, at most, a small supporting accent.
- Use raspberry for recognisable headings and calls to action; green as an occasional alternative panel or leaf detail, not the default background of the whole feed.
- Gold details should be small and flat-colour, not sparkling or metallic gradients.
- Use no more than the two brand typefaces. Keep type large, with comfortable margins. Prefer a real photo to busy ornamentation.
- Include a discreet name or wordmark when useful, not a giant watermark on every post.
- Build posts with consistent spacing and type treatments, but vary crop, photography and layout so the feed stays human.
- Accessible captions, high-contrast text, and informative alternative text where supported matter more than decorative consistency.

### Content pillars (adapt to actual services)

- Practical information: how appointments work, where to go, what to wear or expect.
- Clear explanations of the massage services she **offers at launch**.
- Introducing Maija as a qualified practitioner, her verified credentials, working approach and practice space.
- Seasonal or everyday wellbeing observations tied to her actual work, without pseudo-medical claims.
- Pregnancy-related treatments and breastfeeding support only if relevant qualifications, scope and availability are verified for launch.

## 8. Three things to protect as the brand develops

1. **Freshness:** light space and sunlit natural photography; not an enclosed, aromatic-spa atmosphere.
2. **The green's supporting role:** Maija specifically wants green in the palette but does not want it to dominate or feel heavy.
3. **Authenticity and professional credibility:** this is a personal massage business, not a generic goddess-wellness brand. The real Maija, her actual skills and her clients' practical needs should be visible.
